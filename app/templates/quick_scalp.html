What this is: rebuilt 4 Sep 2026 -- a VWAP 2σ mean-reversion scalp, replacing the earlier EMA/RSI-crossover build entirely. Each completed 1-minute bar is checked against a session VWAP and its 2σ bands: a bar that pierces a band, closes back inside it, leaves a rejection wick covering ≥30% of its range, and has RSI(7) confirming exhaustion (<30 on a lower-band piercing, >70 on an upper one) arms a setup. If the very next completed bar trades back through that setup bar's opposite extreme, that's the entry trigger -- a Deep ITM contract (~100 points from spot, opposite direction from a plain ATM pick) is bought and split 50/50 into a Target-1 leg (a flat option-point target) and a Runner leg that rides until spot reaches back to VWAP.

Risk, checked every cycle: a flat option-premium stop (~9 points) enforced by the same 30-second backstop every strategy here uses; a structural index-level stop (the setup bar's own rejection extreme ±1 point, capped at 14 points from the trigger price) checked independently against the live spot; a hard 3-minute time stop if neither leg has resolved; and a breakeven move on the Runner leg's stop once Target 1 has been banked.

What this is not: validated, or running on live tick data. Built to implement the pasted spec's logic exactly -- the VWAP/sigma math, the setup-and-trigger state machine, the dual-leg risk construction -- but this codebase's real data plumbing is 1-minute REST candle polling (the same architecture every other strategy here uses), not a raw WebSocket tick stream, so bar-by-bar the logic is identical but "finalize on the first tick of the next second" isn't literal. Volume for the VWAP bands comes from the underlying's futures contract (index instruments themselves report zero volume); falls back to an equal-weighted variance, per the spec's own contingency, when a futures contract can't be resolved. Paper trading only -- no live-order code path exists for this strategy, so "hard broker-level SL-M" is a fast, deterministic paper stop, not a real exchange order.
